How Governments Are
Weakening Encryption
— And Why It Matters

A documented history of backdoors, legal pressure, and secret deals — and why true zero-knowledge is the only defense left.

For decades, governments around the world have quietly — and sometimes openly — pressured tech companies to weaken or bypass encryption. The goal is always the same: access to your private data. The methods range from secret court orders to public legislation that forces companies to build backdoors.

1. The UK’s War on Encryption (2023–2026)

In 2023–2026, the UK government repeatedly attempted to force Apple to disable or weaken its strongest encryption features. Most notably, they compelled Apple to disable Advanced Data Protection (ADP) for British iCloud users in 2026 — citing national security concerns.

Sources: The Guardian, Reuters (May 2026)

This is not an isolated incident. The UK’s Online Safety Act and previous attempts to break end-to-end encryption in messaging apps (including iMessage and WhatsApp) show a consistent pattern of eroding digital privacy under the guise of protecting citizens.

2. The United States: EARN IT Act & Backdoors

The EARN IT Act (introduced multiple times since 2020) would require tech companies to scan all user content for child sexual abuse material (CSAM) — effectively creating a backdoor that undermines end-to-end encryption.

Sources: Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF), The New York Times

Privacy experts and companies like Signal, WhatsApp, and Apple have strongly opposed the bill, warning that it would make mass surveillance easier and weaken security for everyone — including children.

3. European Union: Chat Control (CSAM Regulation)

The EU’s proposed Chat Control regulation would require messaging apps to scan all private messages — including end-to-end encrypted ones — for illegal content. This would effectively ban true end-to-end encryption across the EU.

Sources: European Parliament, Patrick Breyer (MEP)

4. Australia’s TOLA Act (2018)

Australia’s Telecommunications and Other Legislation Amendment Act (TOLA) allows government agencies to secretly compel tech companies to build backdoors into their products — without public knowledge or oversight.

Sources: Human Rights Watch, The Guardian Australia
