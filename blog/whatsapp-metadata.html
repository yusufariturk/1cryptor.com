WhatsApp Encrypts Your Messages — But Meta Knows Everything Else About You

Ongoing 5 min read 4 sources
WhatsApp Privacy Reality
WhatsApp's messages are end-to-end encrypted using the Signal Protocol — but Meta collects extensive metadata about your communications and stores it. WhatsApp also shares data with Meta's advertising infrastructure. Your message content may be private, but everything around it is not.

What WhatsApp Actually Collects

WhatsApp uses the Signal Protocol — the gold standard of end-to-end encrypted messaging — to protect the content of messages in transit. Meta cannot read the text of your messages. This part is genuine and technically sound.

What WhatsApp's privacy marketing does not emphasize is what surrounds those messages. According to WhatsApp's own Privacy Policy, the app collects: your phone number, device identifiers, IP address, mobile network information, browser information, operating system, app version, time zone, and geolocation data. More revealing is behavioral metadata: who you communicate with, at what times, with what frequency, for how long, in which groups. This metadata is shared with Meta Companies and used as a signal in Meta's advertising and content recommendation systems.

The 2021 privacy policy update made this data sharing explicit and mandatory. Users who declined the updated policy were threatened with loss of access.

Metadata Is Often More Revealing Than Content

In 2014, former NSA Director Michael Hayden stated bluntly: "We kill people based on metadata." Metadata analysis can reveal which doctor you've been messaging, which political organizations you communicate with, unusual contact patterns suggesting a new relationship or crisis, and location data that reconstructs your physical movements. None of this requires reading a single word you've typed.

WhatsApp's E2E encryption protects your message text — but the metadata surrounding every message (who, when, how often, from where) is collected by Meta and shared across the Meta advertising network. Your communication patterns are a product being sold.
