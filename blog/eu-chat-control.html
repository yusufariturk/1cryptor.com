EU Chat Control: How Europe Almost Mandated Mass Surveillance of Every Private Message and File

2022–2024 6 min read 5 sources
2022–2024 Legislative Battle
From 2022 to 2024, the European Commission pushed a regulation that would require all messaging apps and cloud storage services to scan every private message and file for illegal content using "client-side scanning." Privacy experts, cryptographers, and even the EU Parliament called it a mass surveillance system incompatible with fundamental rights.

What Chat Control Proposed

In May 2022, the European Commission introduced a proposal formally titled the Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) Regulation — quickly nicknamed "Chat Control" by critics and privacy advocates. The mechanism at its heart was client-side scanning (CSS): apps would scan the content of every message and file on the user's device, before encryption was applied, comparing content against a government-maintained database of prohibited material.

The cryptography research community was nearly unanimous in condemnation. An open letter signed by 465 scientists and cryptography researchers stated that client-side scanning is technically indistinguishable from a backdoor. The proposal also covered cloud storage — any service storing files on behalf of European users would be required to scan uploaded files against the prohibited content database.

Why It's Not Over

In June 2024, the EU Parliament's Civil Liberties Committee voted 41–23 to reject the Chat Control proposal in its current form. But the European Commission retains the authority to revise and reintroduce it. More immediately, a near-identical threat is already law in the United Kingdom — the Online Safety Act (2023) contains provisions that could compel messaging apps and cloud services to implement content scanning. Signal and WhatsApp have publicly stated they would exit the UK market rather than comply.
