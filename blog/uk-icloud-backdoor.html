UK Government Forces Apple to Disable iCloud End-to-End Encryption — What This Means for You

February 2025 6 min read 4 sources
Breaking — February 2025
In February 2025, the UK Home Office issued a secret order under the Investigatory Powers Act demanding Apple create a backdoor into iCloud Advanced Data Protection. Apple responded by disabling the feature entirely for UK users — removing end-to-end encryption for millions of people.

What the UK Government Ordered

Britain's Investigatory Powers Act 2016 — nicknamed the "Snoopers' Charter" — grants the Home Secretary sweeping powers to issue Technical Capability Notices (TCNs) in secret. These notices can compel any company operating in the UK to build surveillance access into their products. Recipients are legally prohibited from disclosing that such a notice exists.

In early 2025, the Home Office demanded something extraordinary: that Apple build a backdoor giving UK intelligence services the ability to access any iCloud account, anywhere in the world. Rather than comply, Apple disabled Advanced Data Protection (ADP) for all UK accounts on February 21, 2025 — with no prior warning to users.

Advanced Data Protection had provided end-to-end encryption for iCloud Backup, Photos, Notes, Reminders, Safari bookmarks, and 9 other data categories. With ADP disabled, Apple regained the technical ability to access this data — and so did anyone with a lawful request to Apple.

Why This Matters Beyond the UK

The Home Office order targeted Apple's global iCloud infrastructure — meaning had Apple complied, the backdoor would have exposed accounts in the United States, Germany, Japan, and everywhere else. One government's legal demand would have become a vulnerability for the entire world.

Australia's Assistance and Access Act, India's proposed data access frameworks, and the EU's discussions around lawful access all demonstrate the same political appetite. When one democracy successfully compels a tech company to weaken encryption, it establishes precedent for every other government to follow.

Apple's standard iCloud encryption lets Apple access your photos, files, and backups. UK law enforcement can now request this data with a standard legal process — no secret order required.
