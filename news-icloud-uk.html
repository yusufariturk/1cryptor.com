UK Government Forces Apple to
Disable iCloud Advanced Data Protection

In a move that has shocked privacy advocates, the UK government has compelled Apple to disable its strongest encryption feature for British iCloud users — citing national security concerns.

James Ball
Technology Editor, The Guardian

In a significant escalation of the ongoing battle between privacy and national security, the UK government has forced Apple to disable Advanced Data Protection (ADP) for iCloud users in Britain. The move, confirmed by multiple sources including The Guardian and Reuters, means that millions of British citizens will no longer have access to Apple's strongest encryption offering.

What This Actually Means

Advanced Data Protection was Apple's most robust encryption layer. It encrypted almost all of your iCloud data — including photos, files, and backups — with end-to-end encryption that even Apple could not access. With ADP disabled, your data reverts to standard encryption, meaning Apple (and potentially government agencies) can access your files when legally compelled.

This is not theoretical.

The UK government has a long history of pressuring tech companies to weaken encryption. In 2023, they attempted to force Apple to create a backdoor in iMessage. This latest move is part of a broader pattern of eroding digital privacy under the guise of national security.

Why This Matters for You

Your private photos, sensitive documents, financial records, and personal messages stored in iCloud are no longer protected by the highest level of encryption. Even if you trust Apple, the reality is that once data is accessible to one entity, it becomes vulnerable to others — whether through legal requests, data breaches, or insider threats.

Your Data is Now More Accessible

Apple can be legally required to hand over your iCloud data to UK authorities — and potentially share it with other governments through intelligence-sharing agreements.

Big Tech Still Profits From Your Data
